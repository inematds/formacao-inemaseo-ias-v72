Lesson 8 · 18 min

Agent commerce and the monthly measurement loop

Agents already fill in forms and compare products by reading the page the way a screen reader does. What helps is the basics done well: semantic HTML, labels, correct product data. And none of it counts without a routine: a monthly loop with the dashboards, the battery, the log and the score, always with a date.

02 · Four tweaks that serve people and agents

Click each step. All of them come from web.dev's guide to agent-friendly sites.

1. Real elements

An action is a button, navigation is a link. If you can't use the right element, give it the correct role and tabindex.

Use a hand cursor (cursor: pointer) on what can be clicked: it's a strong signal of an action.

2. Linked labels

Every field has a visible label tied to it with the for attribute. Email, name, and phone with autocomplete: it helps the person filling in the form and helps the agent know what each field is.

3. Nothing hidden

No transparent layers over buttons; action elements visible and at a minimum size. A layout that doesn't move around after loading: an agent that reads a screenshot gets lost.

4. Product data

A correct Merchant Center feed, a clear return policy, and an up-to-date Business Profile. Follow agent commerce protocols without implementing them: sources still disagree about their status.

Simulation: illustrative example
  • A button made from a generic box with a JavaScript click handler, with no role or label.
  • A field whose label is only placeholder text inside it (it disappears when the person types).
  • Measuring once and stopping: without the next month, there's nothing to compare.

The AIV method suggests: after 3 months with no change in the battery, the lever is no longer your site's content but your presence where the AIs already look (the sources that show up in your place). Treat it as a hypothesis to test, with the loop measuring.
