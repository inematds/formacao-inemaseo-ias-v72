AEO and GEO without the myths: what's official and what's a fad

AEO (optimizing for answer engines) and GEO (optimizing for generative AI) have become a package sold with a file, markup, and a promise. In May 2026, Google published a guide that takes much of that apart. This lesson uses that guide as a yardstick to decide where your time is worth spending.

Start the lesson

  2. 2Why AEO and GEO are well-done SEO measured on a different dashboard
  3. 3How to respond to anyone selling llms.txt as a shortcut
  4. 4Find the content on your site that only you can write

Goal: separate what Google, Bing, and the AIs officially say from what's a fad, and choose where to spend your time.

02 · Four claims checked against the source

Click each one. All were read on the official pages on 10/19/2026.

1. "You need a file for AI"

You don't. Google's AI features page says you don't need to create machine-readable files, text files for AI, or special markup, and that there is no special schema.org to add.

Structured data is still useful when it matches the visible text, just as it was before.

2. "llms.txt gets you into ChatGPT"

The largest public study (Ahrefs, 06/15/2026, 137 thousand domains) found 97% of valid llms.txt files had no requests at all in the month. Google's guide says you can ignore it.

Honest read: llms.txt is optional and cheap, useful as an index for coding agents, and not a citation lever.

3. "A 40–60 word answer is the technique"

No source supports that length as a factor. A direct answer at the start of a section is good for readers; treat it as clarity, not an AI trick.

4. "Bing doesn't matter"

It matters twice: Bing feeds Copilot and has the only official AI citations dashboard. And a Seer study found 87% of ChatGPT citations in Bing's top 20; it's a small study, not a statement from OpenAI. Lesson 5.

A question to find your non-commodity content
List 3 things my business knows that no competitor or AI could write on their own:
1. a number only I have (e.g.: real average delivery time, price charged, measured result)
2. an experience only I have lived (e.g.: a case, a mistake, a before and after)
3. an opinion I defend with a reason
For each one: which page on my site already says it? If none does, which page would be the owner page?
